1. Executive Summary

Founded in October 2013 by Jeremy Allaire and Sean Neville, Circle is the second-largest stablecoin issuer and operator of the USDC ecosystem, now expanding into its own Layer-1 blockchain (Arc), a payments network (CPN), and agentic commerce infrastructure. Q2 2026 showed a revenue miss ($701M vs $717M expected) and a declining reserve return rate (3.5%, -66 bps YoY). The stock fell ~3% on the day after an initial ~7% pre-market rally, trading as low as $59.12. However, the quarter also delivered $48M in net income (vs -$482M loss a year ago), record average USDC circulation ($76.5B), a 151% YoY surge in on-chain transaction volume, and a 41% jump in other revenue. The Arc mainnet launches September 16, with a confirmed founding validator cohort of 11 institutions. Circle's federal trust bank charter is a structural differentiator.

Revised Verdict: Cautious Buy Event-Driven Binary-Weighted

Circle is a two-asset entity: (1) a regulated reserve-income business (~95% of revenue) with decelerating fundamentals, and (2) Arc protocol optionality that's partially priced. Sequential deterioration in Q2 (flat revenue vs Q1, shrinking circulation, lower EPS) makes the core yield business a deteriorating asset, not a stable floor. At the same time, the Arc validator cohort is confirmed and substantially broader than initially reported, de-risking the launch narrative.

Downside: Morgan Stanley's $38 Underweight target (Aug 3) sits below the prior $45 floor, citing weaker USDC supply forecasts and competition from tokenized cash products. The real downside anchor is ~$38-45, implying 30-42% drawdown from current levels.

Upside: TD Cowen initiated at $82 Buy. Long-side models project $120+ by 2028 if Arc captures meaningful revenue. The Arc fee capture mechanism remains the critical unresolved unknown.

Positioning: This is an event-driven trade around September 16. Decelerating core fundamentals and a 95% rate-concentrated revenue base argue against full conviction. Enter with initial sizing, scale only after Arc fee distribution model is clarified. The report's own advice applies to the initial position, not just to scaling.

⚠️ Tether USA₮ Analysis: Tether's U.S.-compliant stablecoin directly targets Circle's institutional segment. Competitive threat is real but nuanced:

  • USA₮'s advantage: Tether's $185B supply creates liquidity depth. If USA₮ achieves Treasury-backed transparency while inheriting Tether's liquidity, institutions may not need Circle's regulatory moat.
  • Circle's defense: Federal trust charter, MiCA compliance, GENIUS Act alignment create a regulatory stack requiring years to replicate.

⚠️ Arc Presale / Other Revenue Correction: The v2 report incorrectly claimed Q2's $34M other revenue "likely includes proceeds from the $242M presale." This is wrong. Per the earnings call, presale revenue is recognized as product milestones are achieved, with ~$180M included in the revised 2026 full-year guide — i.e., recognition is forward-looking, not back-loaded into Q2. Q2's $34M is actual recurring other revenue. The +41% YoY growth from $24M in Q2 2025 is real recurring growth, not presale-inflated. However, the full-year $310-330M guidance does include ~$180M from the presale. Excluding presale, the sustainable other revenue run-rate for 2026 is $130-150M, not $310-330M. This is the correct way to separate one-time from recurring.

9. Valuation Assessment

Current: ~$16.9B market cap at ~$59-67/share (intraday low $59.12 on earnings day).

The Two-Asset Framework

Asset 1: Reserve-Income Business (Decelerating)

  • ~$700M annualized reserve income, declining as Fed cuts
  • Sequential deterioration in Q2: revenue flat QoQ, EBITDA down 5%, circulation down 4.8%
  • ⚠️ Downside anchor revised to $38-55. Morgan Stanley's $38 reflects a scenario where tokenized cash products erode USDC balances and reserve income compounds downward. Mizuho at $45. The prior $45-60 floor was too generous.
  • This is not a "steady toll-booth" — it's a declining toll-booth in a rate-cut cycle with emerging competitors.

Asset 2: Arc Protocol Optionality (Partially Priced)

  • Binary catalyst: Sept 16 mainnet launch
  • 11 confirmed founding validators de-risks the launch narrative — ⚠️ but doesn't solve the fee capture question
  • If Arc succeeds AND Circle captures protocol fees: multiple expansion to 20-40x, potential $87-120+ price
  • If Arc succeeds but Circle captures only incremental USDC volume: modest upside, Arc helps reserve income but doesn't create new revenue category
  • If Arc underperforms: stock re-rates to Asset 1 value ($38-55)
  • Current price implies ~$10-25 of Arc optionality priced in

The Morgan Stanley Challenge: MS's thesis directly attacks Asset 1's floor. Their scenario: tokenized money market funds and tokenized bank deposits compete with USDC for institutional cash balances. If USDC supply grows slower than expected (-33% MS forecast for 2027, -44% for 2028), reserve income declines faster than consensus models. This isn't just a "rates are cutting" story — it's a "rates are cutting AND the base is shrinking" story.

The Counterargument: The 11-institution Arc validator cohort is a direct rebuttal to MS's claim that "stablecoin utility beyond remittances has not gained traction." BlackRock, DTCC, Visa, Mastercard, ICE, Global Payments, Standard Chartered, and others are building on Arc. This is traction. ⚠️ But it's also what MS calls "test participation" — institutions showing up for the photo. The question is whether it converts to sustained volume and, critically, whether Circle captures the resulting fees.

Valuation Gap: The market is pricing Circle as a stablecoin toll-booth with Arc optionality. It's not pricing Arc as a new revenue platform (because the fee mechanism is unresolved). It's not fully pricing the Morgan Stanley bear case (stock is 75-76% above $38). The gap exists because the Arc binary hasn't resolved yet.

10. Verdict

Rating: Cautious Buy Event-Driven Binary-Weighted Sept 16 Catalyst

⚠️ The thesis has shifted materially from v1 and v2. The Morgan Stanley $38 target is the single most impactful development since the last report. It sits below the prior floor and directly challenges the two-asset framework's downside anchor. Combined with sequential deterioration in Q2 (decelerating growth, shrinking circulation, lower EPS and EBITDA), the risk profile is worse than either prior version reflected.

What's worse:

  • Morgan Stanley at $38 (was: only Mizuho at $45)
  • Sequential deterioration: revenue flat QoQ, circulation down 4.8%, EBITDA down 5%
  • The bear case is a single macro regime (rate cuts + shrinking circulation + Tether competition), not independent risks
  • Arc fee capture mechanism still unresolved after debate and review
  • DTCC tokenization won't start until H2 2027 — longer-dated than implied

What's better:

  • Arc validator cohort is confirmed and substantial (11 institutions, not 4)
  • Coinbase agreement renewed on existing terms
  • Q2's +41% other revenue growth is real recurring growth (presale was NOT back-loaded into Q2)
  • Arc is de-risked on the launch side, even if the revenue side remains unclear

Downside: ~$38 (Morgan Stanley) to $45 (Mizuho). From current ~$59-67, that's 30-42% drawdown.

Upside: ~$82 (TD Cowen) to $120+ (long-side models). From current, that's 22-85% upside.

Asymmetry remains, but the floor has moved down.

Positioning guidance:

  • Initial sizing: Small. The core business is decelerating. The option is real but binary. The fee capture question is unresolved.
  • Scale triggers: Arc genesis validator list publication, Arc fee distribution model disclosure, post-mainnet volume data.
  • Key monitors: Morgan Stanley thesis development (is USDC supply actually shrinking faster?), Tether USA₮ market share trajectory, Fed rate trajectory.
  • This is not a "buy and hold" stock. It's an event-driven trade around Arc. Enter small, scale on clarity, cut if the fee capture mechanism proves unfavorable.

The honest answer: The report's own advice in v2 was "wait for Arc's genesis validator list and fee distribution model before increasing position size." With the Morgan Stanley downgrade and sequential deterioration, that advice now applies to the initial position too. The option is worth buying, but the right way to buy it is small, event-driven sizing — not full conviction.
